Every job I have had was the same job: take a messy real world signal and return one thing a person can act on.

The signal kept changing shape. It was a noisy ECG lead in a cardiology clinic. Then a retina, then a patch of skin. Then a doctor's handwriting. Then a warehouse camera and the people moving through it. Then a face at a gate. Then a firehose of news, which is when I stopped taking the problem to work and started a company around it.

The company was NewzTiQ. I ran it for two years and closed it in June 2026. Now the signal is a model's own output, and the thing I am building reads it: coding agents, small and fast, written in Rust, with security treated as a design constraint rather than a patch.

I am a machine learning engineer by training and a shipper by preference. Six companies, one of my own, and a decade of taking models from a notebook to something running on a GPU that someone is paying for.

Hybris

Rust

An agent built from scratch for small and local models. A frontier model can recover from its own bad step, so the harness around it can afford to be loose. A small model cannot, so Hybris does that work in the scaffolding: tight tool contracts, explicit state, recovery designed in rather than assumed. The target is a model running on your own machine finishing the task a frontier model finishes.

ZapClaw

Rust

An agent runtime built security first. Bound to localhost by default instead of every interface, workspace confined and symlink safe, sandboxed with whatever the platform already provides, and layered guards against prompt injection. The company

NewzTiQ

June 2024 to June 2026 / closed

I left 56 Secure in April 2024 and started NewzTiQ that June. The idea was narrow on purpose. Instead of an infinite feed, a fixed daily digest. You told it how much time you had and what you cared about, it gave you exactly that much, summarized at the depth you asked for, and then it stopped.

Read exactly what you need, nothing more. The feed was never going to say that, so I built something that would.

I built all of it. An ingestion and classification pipeline running across hundreds of sources every morning. An editorial ranking layer that decided what actually mattered that day. Summarization at several depth levels, so the same story could be thirty seconds or five minutes. A deep research agent for going underneath a headline. A video digest generator. A Flutter app, signed and shipped. The web app, the landing pages, and a set of marketing agents that ran growth while I worked on the product.

It closed in June 2026. The product did what it promised. The habit it was fighting, the scroll, turned out to be stronger than the fix, and I would rather write that down plainly than dress it up as a pivot. Two years of owning every layer alone taught me more about shipping than the six years before it did, and it is the reason I now start with the runtime instead of the model.
